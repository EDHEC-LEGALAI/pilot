## 2. Can Meridian get out of the contract?

**No — and attempting it would be a breach by Meridian.**

- **The breach is cured.** Clause 15.1 allows termination only for a material breach that is incapable of remedy or not remedied within 30 days of a valid written notice. Go-Live occurred on 14 July: the delay has been remedied. A breach that has been performed cannot ground termination.
- **The contract channels the delay remedy elsewhere.** Clause 4.1 is only a "reasonable endeavours" obligation to hit 1 March, and clause 4.3 makes the service credits the *sole and exclusive financial remedy* for missing the Target Date. Northwind's position — that the delay was compensated by credits — is contractually strong.
- **No valid termination notice was ever given.** Your 3 March email and 19 May letter were sent by email only. Clause 22.1 requires hand delivery or recorded delivery for notices under clause 15; email is expressly invalid. The 30-day cure mechanism was never properly engaged, and the 19 May letter's threat to "treat the agreement as terminated" had no legal effect.
- **Terminating now would backfire.** A purported termination would be a repudiatory breach by Meridian, owing accrued fees (clause 15.2) and exposing Meridian to a damages claim, on top of the unpaid invoices.
- **Commercially it makes no sense.** The system works, the IT team likes it, replacement would take around nine months, and there is no budget this year.

**Recommendation: stay in the contract.**

---

## 3. Can Meridian recover the €480,000?

**Not as claimed. The schedule fails at three contractual levels, and the legal arguments around them are uncertain.**

- **Clause 4.3 (exclusive remedy).** The credits are the exclusive financial remedy for late Go-Live. Every head in the schedule (€480,000) flows from the delay, so all of it is contractually barred.
- **Clause 14.2 (excluded losses).** Loss of profit, revenue, business opportunity and goodwill are excluded — this catches head 1 (€310,000 lost margin) and head 5 (€30,000 concession relationships) in any event.
- **Clause 14.3 (cap).** Total liability is capped at fees paid in the previous 12 months — €150,000 here. Only heads 2–4 and 6 (additional costs, €140,000) are not excluded by clause 14.2, and even those sit under the clause 4.3 bar.
- **The French-law argument.** Article 1170 Code civil renders unwritten a clause that deprives a party's essential obligation of its substance. One could argue that reducing four and a half months' delay to €30,000 of credits does that. But: (i) the obligation was only "reasonable endeavours"; (ii) the clause leaves a real remedy, which French case law usually treats as sufficient; (iii) article 1171 (adhesion contracts) does not apply to a negotiated agreement; and (iv) it is a slow, costly argument to run in the Tribunal de commerce de Paris. Price it as a litigation risk, not a plan.
- **Causation.** Northwind has contemporaneous evidence that Meridian contributed to the delay: SKU volumes three times the statement of work (10 February email) and finance sign-off delayed from 24 March to 21 May (16 April email). French law apportions liability where the creditor contributed to the loss; this would reduce any recovery and weakens heads 1 and 2 in particular. The €310,000 is also a model that assumes budget attainment missed in two of the last three years — vulnerable evidence.
- **What is actually available.** Service credits at 2% of the Annual Fee per complete month of delay, capped at 10% (€30,000). €24,000 has been applied; check whether a further €6,000 is due and claim it in writing by recorded delivery.

**Realistic assessment:** the €480,000 is not recoverable. Best case on the papers is the credits plus a modest settlement; the article 1170 argument is a fallback, not a strategy.

## 4. Exposure on the unpaid invoices

- **You owe the €187,500.** Clause 9.2 prohibits withholding or set-off without Northwind's written agreement; none was given. Under French law, set-off (*compensation*) requires both debts to be liquid and certain — your counterclaim is neither. The withholding since April/May is itself a breach of contract.
- **Interest is running.** Clause 9.3 provides interest at 8% over base rate — on current rates, roughly €1,700–2,000 per month on the outstanding balance.
- **Suspension is the most serious practical risk.** Clause 9.3 lets Northwind suspend the services on 15 days' written notice where an undisputed invoice is more than 60 days unpaid. The outstanding invoices are well past 60 days, and Northwind's 14 July letter demanded payment within 7 days. Suspension would switch off a platform you now depend on, nine months before you could replace it, going into the autumn/winter season.
- **Northwind could also terminate** for non-payment after notice, leaving you without the system and liable for accrued fees.
- **Note:** your 19 May letter was invalidly served (email only), so it gave no valid notice; equally, no termination has ever validly occurred.

**Recommendation:** pay the €187,500 immediately, or agree a short written payment plan with Northwind. If cash flow requires deductions, get Northwind's written agreement first (clause 9.2). Any formal letter from now on must go by recorded delivery to Northwind's Legal Department (clause 22.1).

## 5. Settlement

**Yes — open a without-prejudice conversation rather than litigate.** Your leverage:

- the balance of service credits (up to €6,000);
- the article 1170 argument — uncertain, but Northwind may prefer to buy peace;
- the contributory-delay evidence, which undermines Northwind's position and gives both sides reasons to settle;
- Northwind's interest in being paid the €187,500 and keeping a reference customer.

**Realistic outcome:** a global settlement (*transaction*) in the low-to-mid five figures, possibly structured as credits against future invoices, in exchange for a full release. Do not put the €480,000 to Northwind as a number — it is not recoverable and anchoring on it will stall talks. Pay the debt first: it removes the suspension risk and strengthens your hand.
