**Advice note — Meridian Retail Group SAS / Northwind Systems Limited**

**1. Answers**

**Can we get out of the contract? No — and you should not want to.** The only route is clause 15.1 (material breach incapable of remedy, or unremedied 30 days after written notice). No compliant notice was ever served — the 3 March and 19 May emails fail clause 22.1 (hand or recorded delivery, marked for the Legal Department) — and the delay was remedied when the platform went live on 14 July 2025. Northwind would add that clause 4.1 was only a reasonable-endeavours obligation. Commercially, staying is right: the system works, IT likes it, and replacement would take nine months with no budget this year.

**Can we recover the EUR 480,000? No.** Clause 4.3 makes the clause 4.2 service credits the sole and exclusive financial remedy for late Go-Live. Those credits — 2% of the EUR 300,000 annual fee per complete month of delay, capped at 10% — total EUR 24,000 for the four complete months (March–June), and Northwind has already applied them. Heads 1 and 5 (EUR 340,000) are in any event excluded by clause 14.2 (loss of profit, revenue, business opportunity, goodwill). The remaining heads (EUR 140,000) fail on clause 4.3. An attack on clause 4.3 under article 1170 Code civil is possible but uncertain, slow and expensive, and Northwind has contributory-fault facts (your SKU data of 20 January; finance's reconciliation sign-off delayed to 21 May).

**How exposed are we on the unpaid invoices? Significantly.** Clause 9.2 prohibits withholding without Northwind's written agreement, so withholding since May is itself a breach. Northwind is owed EUR 187,500; it can charge interest at 8% above base and may suspend on 15 days' written notice once an undisputed invoice is 60 days unpaid — a condition already met. Its 14 July email is not a valid suspension notice under clause 22.1, so you have a short window, but treat the threat as real.

- 10 Feb 2025 — Northwind flags slippage; blames the volume of Meridian's SKU data (20 January).

- 3 Mar / 19 May 2025 — Meridian emails purporting to give notice and terminate; neither complies with clause 22.1.

**3. Analysis**

*Termination (question 1).* Clause 4.3 preserves the clause 15 right, but three things defeat it. First, no valid notice: clause 22.1 requires hand delivery or recorded delivery, and email is invalid for clause 15; neither the 3 March "formal notice" nor the 19 May letter qualifies. Second, the 19 May letter allowed no 30-day cure period as clause 15.1 requires. Third, the breach is remedied: a delay is capable of remedy, and performance occurred on 14 July 2025. Even if termination were arguable, clause 15.2 would oblige Meridian to pay all accrued fees — exit would cost money and leave you without a platform.

*Quantum (question 2).* Head by head:
- Head 1, lost gross margin EUR 310,000: excluded by clause 14.2 and clause 4.3. Not recoverable. Finance's own caveat — budget was missed in two of the last three years — makes causation weak in any event.
- Heads 2, 3, 4 and 6 (EUR 140,000): extra costs, not caught by clause 14.2, but excluded by the clause 4.3 sole-remedy clause. Recoverable only if clause 4.3 were held unwritten under article 1170 Code civil — a real but uncertain argument, because the clause leaves a genuine remedy (credits up to EUR 30,000) and clause 4.1 was only an obligation of means.
- Head 5, concession partners EUR 30,000: excluded by clause 14.2 (business opportunity/goodwill) and clause 4.3.
- Service credits: EUR 24,000 (4 complete months × 2% × EUR 300,000) already received — the full contractual entitlement. The clause 14.3 cap (fees paid in the prior 12 months, EUR 150,000) is not engaged.

If clause 4.3 were attacked and fell, realistic recovery on heads 2–4 and 6 after a contributory-fault reduction would be perhaps EUR 60,000–100,000, against EUR 75,000–150,000 of costs and 12–24 months in the Tribunal de commerce de Paris. Not worth running.

*Non-payment (question 3).* Clause 9.2 makes the withholding a breach independent of the delay dispute. Under clause 9.3 Northwind may suspend on 15 days' written notice once an undisputed invoice is 60 days unpaid; the oldest unpaid invoices pass that mark in August. Suspension mid-trading would cost more than the dispute is worth. Northwind could also terminate for non-payment under clause 15.1, triggering clause 15.2 (all accrued fees) — the worst commercial outcome.

- Assumed no written set-off agreement exists; if one does, the withholding may be lawful.
